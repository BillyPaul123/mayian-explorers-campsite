## 2. Chapter 01 — `14.10.26,.jpeg`

> **Morning in the Manyatta**
>
> The day began with the soft chime of cowbells and the gentle lowing of
> cattle — the lifeblood and wealth of the Samburu people. Guests at Mayian woke
> early, stepping out of their tents to join the village elders and herders.
>
> Guided by local Samburu hosts draped in brilliant crimson shúkàs and intricate
> beadwork, visitors walked through the thorny acacia enclosure (kraal) of the
> manyatta. They watched as young herders carefully tended to their livestock,
> learning how milk and blood sustained generations through drought and bloom,
> and how deep respect for the land dictated every seasonal migration.
>
> Just a short walk from the camp lay the pristine wilderness. With binoculars
> in hand, guests joined an early morning bird walk. The dry thorn scrub buzzed
> with life as vibrant lilac-breasted rollers, hornbills, and glossy starlings
> darted between branches.

## 3. Chapter 02 — `14.10.26.jpeg`

> **Into the Wild Samburu**
>
> Just four kilometers away lay the famed Samburu National Reserve, a sanctuary
> like no other. Leaving the manyatta behind, the safari vehicle wound its way
> toward the Ewaso Ng'iro River.
>
> Samburu was famous for the "Big Five," but it was the Samburu Special Five
> that truly held the crowd spellbound:
> - The long-necked Gerenuk, standing on its hind legs to reach high acacia leaves.
> - The regal Grevy's Zebra, with its fine, narrow stripes.
> - The bold, long-horned Beisa Oryx.
> - The majestic Somali Ostrich, displaying its blue-tinted neck.
> - The intricate net-like patterns of the Reticulated Giraffe.
